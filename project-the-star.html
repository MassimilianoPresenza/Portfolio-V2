About the project

Immersive Story is a fourth-year elective at CMD Maastricht about storytelling through Virtual Reality. The assignment: take an existing short story and turn it into a VR experience, while staying as close as possible to the meaning of the original. It doesn't have to be a literal translation. The goal is to use what makes VR special (spatiality, immersion, presence, agency and embodiment) to tell the story in a new way.

I chose The Star by H. G. Wells and built a prototype in Unreal Engine 5. The course went from story analysis and VR design theory, through concepting and concept art, to paper prototypes and a high-fidelity prototype.

The original story

In Wells' story, a wandering body from outside the solar system collides with Neptune. The two merge into a burning mass that is pulled toward the Sun, swung by Jupiter's gravity, and passes close to the Earth. A mathematician warns the world and is laughed at. The Earth is flooded, shaken and scorched, but survives.

The story ends with Martian astronomers who barely notice any damage. From millions of miles away, the greatest human catastrophe looks small. That detached, distant viewpoint became the starting point for my adaptation.

My adaptation

I wrote my own version of the story as the base for the experience. You are an unseen observer of the cosmos, floating in the Solar System. You watch an ancient asteroid, nicknamed Billy, enter from deep space, get flung and then slowed by the giant planets, until it is on a direct course for Earth.

Then the story hands the decision to you. Humanity prays and promises to change, while a cult screams that people don't deserve Earth. Do they deserve saving? You can intervene, or let nature take its course. Either way, doubt follows: a time rift opens and you get to choose again. You can preview the story, the scene script and my logbook further down.

Why VR fits this story

Wells already tells his story from far away, so I made that distance the player's role. In VR the Solar System becomes a space you stand inside, with planets around you, and the asteroid's path is something you follow with your head and eyes.

Agency is the core of the concept. Wells' mathematician can only warn; the observer can act. The choice (save or let go) and the time rift that lets you undo it turn the question of whether humanity deserves a second chance into something the player has to answer and then live with.

Design choices |

How the VR medium carries the story

Spatiality

The Solar System is a space around you. The camera drifts backward at the key moment, so Earth hangs between you and the asteroid's burning light.

Presence & embodiment

You are an unseen observer, a being of the cosmos. There is no body to play, so the role is carried by scale, silence and distance.

Agency

You can rotate the Earth at will and hear different voices on each side: prayers, acceptance and curses. Then you make the choice: intervene or let go.

Sound & time

A calm, cosmic voice-over guides the experience, with deliberate pauses. A time rift lets the player rewind and choose again after doubt.

What I took away from it

I started in a third-person template, then found that moving assets into a VR project was complicated, so I restarted from the VR template. It cost time, but it made the rest of development smoother and more focused.

Testing in the headset matters more than assumptions. Things I expected to work on a monitor behaved differently in VR, so I tested and adjusted regularly on the real hardware. Lighting, atmosphere and scale did most of the work in making the space feel believable.

The biggest lesson was connecting theory to practice: terms like presence, embodiment and plausibility illusion became concrete design decisions once I had to build them.

My version of the story

The short story I wrote as the base for the experience: the observer, Billy, and the choice.

Excerpt · The Star, MSJ Presenza

Across every continent, people fall to their knees. They are praying, pleading with a "god" they've neglected, a vast, global promise to right their centuries of wrongs: to end the wars, to curb the greed, to heal the scarred body of the Earth. A plea for a second chance.

But another, darker voice also cuts through the panic. A cult, long lurking in the shadows, now stands in the open, screaming their absolute conviction into the void. We don't deserve it! they shriek.

Do they deserve saving?

The fate of a world, and all its hopes and dreams, now rests on the answer to that single, unbearable question. And now, you must decide. Will you save the Earth, or will you let it perish?

If you intervene, the people rejoice, but their promises dissolve and their wars continue, and doubt sets in. If you let Billy go, the end is swift, but you feel the weight of it and wonder whether they could have changed. Either way, a time rift appears and the choice is yours once more.

The experience scene by scene: what the viewer sees and hears.

Scene overview
SceneThe viewer seesThe viewer hears
1 · IntroductionDarkness, then the Milky Way. The Solar System comes into focus and an ancient, spinning asteroid streaks across the black.Calm, cosmic voice-over: you are an unseen observer.
2 · The JourneyThe asteroid is pulled in by Neptune and arcs around Jupiter's storms."It slowed." Its path bends.
3 · The RevelationA glowing trajectory line traces the new course. Earth is revealed, blue and fragile."A single thought could change everything."
4 · The ChoiceYou rotate the Earth and hear different voices on each side. The camera then drifts back with Earth framed against the asteroid's light.Prayers, acceptance and curses, then: "Will you save them, or let the void reclaim what it once owned?"
5 · InterventionA pulse of cosmic energy tilts the asteroid's path. People celebrate, wars resume, and a time rift opens."Was your mercy wasted?"
6 · Letting goThe impact, the Earth breaking apart, then drifting fragments and a new rift."For the first time, you feel… human."

Design logbook

My ten-week process, from first analysis to final presentation.

Process in short
Weeks 1–2Introduction to the minor, VR play sessions and Unreal basics. Chose The Star out of three stories and analysed its themes of cosmic destruction and human insignificance.
Weeks 3–4Concepting with feedback on narrative structure and emotional engagement. VR theory: embodiment, presence, plausibility illusion and the virtual continuum.
Week 5Oral assessment on storytelling theory and the concept presentation.
Week 6Started building in Unreal. Switched from a third-person template to a VR template and experimented with lighting, atmosphere and scale.
Weeks 7–8Creating assets, adjusting textures, refining interactions and testing them in the headset.
Weeks 9–10Intensive build and test sessions, then polishing lighting, sound and pacing before the final presentation.
